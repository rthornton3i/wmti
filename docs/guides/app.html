The app

In short: the app is the whole planner in one web page. It runs the same Python model inside the browser, keeps your scenarios on the phone and sends nothing anywhere. Three tabs at the bottom hold everything: Setup (your scenarios and the household), Inputs (the settings, one chip per group) and Outputs (Summary, Plots, Compare and Optimize). The ? button at the top right gives a short tour of the controls, and Undo at the top left takes back the last edits. This page is for anyone using the app: the pictures, then what every screen does and the final report. The rules behind each settings group are in Model rules by group.

The app, Walk Me Through It (WMTI), runs the real wmti model in the browser with Pyodide, so its numbers are those of a native Python run. It is published as an installable web app on GitHub Pages and as a private claude.ai artifact. It has the app frame, every settings group, the headline figures, the Monte Carlo behind every output (its charts; its tables are in the final report), Compare, Optimize, the final report and saved scenarios on the device.

What the page does

The first load downloads about 20 MB (the calculation libraries and the model) and shows each start-up step with its time; on a phone it takes about 20 seconds, later loads are faster. A bottom tab bar holds three tabs, Setup, Inputs and Outputs; chips and a switch inside pick a view. The look, the menu and the color schemes are under “Look and color scheme” below.

On a window 1100 px wide or wider (a PC monitor) the page switches to a wide layout by itself, live as the window is resized: the top bar becomes one banner across the top with the pinned headline figures, Undo, ? and the menu, and Setup, Inputs and Outputs show side by side below it (one fifth, two fifths and two fifths of the width, but Setup never narrower than 340 px, so on an iPad in landscape Inputs and Outputs share what is left), each down to the bottom of the window and scrolling on its own under its chips rows. There is no bottom bar, pin button or sideways swipe between panels, and the tour skips its bottom-bar and pin steps. A narrower window is the phone layout (wireWide in frontend/src/app/20-layout.js).

In the wide layout a divider runs down between the Inputs and Outputs columns, like the bar between two snapped windows. Drag it (mouse, pen or finger) to give one column more room; each open column keeps at least 280 px, and dragging a column to under half of that closes it, leaving the divider at that edge to be dragged back. Hover over the small icon at the top of the divider (or tab to it) and three buttons pop out: Show only Outputs, Split equally and Show only Inputs. Double-clicking the divider splits equally. From the keyboard the divider is a separator: Left and Right move it, Home shows only Outputs, End only Inputs and Enter splits equally. The split is kept on this device for the next visit (wmti.split.v1, cleared by Clear all data); a link that opens a closed column splits equally again, and the phone layout ignores the split (wireSplit in frontend/src/app/20-layout.js, the split* helpers in frontend/src/shared.js).

Setup can be folded away in the wide layout. Click the bar above its column (“Setup” with a chevron, also reached with Tab and pressed with Enter or Space) and the column shrinks to a 44 px rail showing a chevron and the open scenario’s name; Inputs and Outputs take the freed width at the split they had. Click the rail to bring Setup back. Folding only hides the column, so nothing in it is lost and it reopens where it was scrolled. Anything that goes to Setup unfolds it first: a link to #setup, Undo of a Setup field, the tour’s Setup steps, opening, duplicating or importing a scenario and the export or import message. The choice is kept on this device (wmti.setupFold.v1, cleared by Clear all data); the phone layout has no fold (wireSetupFold in frontend/src/app/20-layout.js).

Inputs

Scenarios on Setup

The settings you edit are the open scenario. Its card at the top of Setup (accent-edged) shows “Open scenario”, the name in large type and a status line: “Unsaved changes” with a dot while the settings differ from the copy saved on this device (or none is saved yet), “Saved” or “Saved just now” once they match, and “Saved automatically” while the small Auto-save switch beside it is on. While the open scenario is the unedited example household, a “Tough example” tag with an (i) under the name says it runs short of money in many runs on purpose (any edit, a rename or a duplicate removes the tag; Load example household brings it back). Auto-save is on by default for every scenario: on a first visit, for a new scenario, a duplicate and an imported file (from Setup’s Import or the walkthrough’s first step). Turned off, it stays off for that scenario only (its name in autoSaveOff in wmti.prefs.v1; a rename takes the choice along, a delete forgets it; the older one-switch autoSave preference is not read). While it is on, every settled edit (a 0.4 s pause) writes the open scenario’s saved copy, creating it under its name if there is none, and the Save button is hidden; a scenario that opens (a new one, a duplicate, an import) is saved under its name at once, so a reload keeps it. An imported file whose name is already a saved scenario with other settings opens as “name*_1” (the next free number) instead of writing over it (``importedName`` in ``frontend/src/shared.js``); only the settings count, so a file that differs only in effort, style or walkthrough progress (or has none, from before the pickers) replaces the saved copy. Under the status line one flat strip of three equal-width icon buttons holds **Rename*, Duplicate and Clear; on a phone narrower than 380 px the labels shrink so they fit one row at 320 px.

Each step then scrolls any sideways chips row to its control, waits for the scroll to settle and only then rings the part of the control that shows. Effort and style

Effort sets how much the app asks: Low (pay, savings and monthly spending), Medium (adds retirement age, the 401k match, home, cars, loans and Social Security) or High (everything). Style sets how results are shown: Accountant (numbers and tables), Coach (plus next steps and tips) or Gamer (plus quests, awards and mini games). Neither changes a result. At Low the Inputs tab is one short page and the walkthrough asks the Low questions only (see Low effort); at Medium each Inputs pane and list sheet shows its Low and Medium inputs with an Add detail row (see Medium effort and Add detail); High shows every input. Coach and Gamer talk like Accountant; the choice is kept so later releases can use it. The choices and their descriptions come from appInfo().experience (EFFORT_CHOICES and STYLE_CHOICES in wmti.dashboard.web).

Walkthrough

“Walk me through it” asks for the main settings one question at a time, over the page. A new user’s first launch (no draft, no saved scenarios and no walkthrough progress in this browser, an older page’s wmti.walk.v1 included) shows, once the engine is ready and after the Terms step, the effort and style picker with Low and Coach chosen and a single Continue button (Escape and a tap outside do nothing), then the walkthrough, then the tour once per device. It opens from the menu item “Walk me through it…” at any time (it reads “Continue the walkthrough…” while one is paused), and from a link ending in #walkthrough. A returning visitor with saved settings sees no walkthrough; until the picker has been kept once on this device (wmti.picker.v1, removed by Clear all data) it shows the picker once after the Terms step, with High and Accountant chosen and the same single Continue, then the app as before. Opening or importing a scenario never opens the walkthrough by itself (a file’s walkthrough left in progress opens paused). Raising the effort while a walkthrough exists offers “Continue the walkthrough…” again, and it opens on the first question the raise unlocked (firstUnlocked in frontend/src/shared.js; the questions are filtered by effort through walkthroughPlan’s effort). Lowering the effort keeps every answer. At Low the welcome reads “About 3 minutes” and the walkthrough asks the Low questions only: 13 for the example household, 9 for a single user with no children (your age, partner, state, pay, savings, the retirement plan, spending, children and the medical plan). Yes on the retirement or medical plan opens its guided pop-up above the walkthrough before the next question, and the plan’s screen then shows its summary with Change (see Low effort). At Medium the welcome reads “About 10 minutes” and the walkthrough asks 35 questions for the example household; at High it reads “As long as you like; stop or skip any time” and asks 39. At Medium a few questions are worded for what Medium covers (MediumWords in wmti.dashboard.walkthrough): the spending offer adds student and other loans (expenses by category is High), the children offer sets college costs (daycare is High), the homes and cars offers and hand-offs name a timeline of owning and renting or leasing (a later purchase or sale is High), and the Social Security question offers only the benefit from your statement: an estimate from pay needs the earnings history, which stays High, as do the last two years of earnings.

A fresh walkthrough on the example household’s settings (a first visit, or after Load example household) first asks How would you like to start?. Start fresh, the main button, goes on to the welcome; Open my file, the quiet button under it, opens the same file picker as Setup’s Import; the file’s settings replace the current ones, named after the file, and the walkthrough closes as finished on the Summary: a scenario file is a whole plan, so every question counts as Answered and none is asked (the menu reopens it on the review), and Undo my answers there puts back the imported settings, never the ones the file replaced. On any other settings the walkthrough opens on the welcome. Whether the step is offered comes from the shared walkthroughPlan (offerImport).

The welcome (under its time estimate) says each answer starts with your current setting, or an example if you have not entered one (the example household on a first visit, your own figures once you have filled some in): change it to yours and tap Continue; Skip keeps what is shown. Start begins; Skip for now closes it for good, opens Outputs and says once that the menu holds it. Hints that need no action now say “later” or “after the walkthrough”; one that points to a setting names it as “Inputs > <group>”. The partner, number of children, number of cars, monthly spending total and account balances answers change the shape of the settings; the cars’ value and loans and the children’s ages write one answer over every car or child. Where surplus cash goes

Each cash, Investments or 529 account has a slider (its share of the surplus, in percent) and a 44 px lock button. Dragging a slider moves the unlocked others so the total stays 100%; a locked account keeps its share and its slider is greyed out. The rule is the Python model’s, not a copy: every move goes to rebalanceSurplus, which calls wmti.dashboard.editing.rebalanceShares and writes the new shares into the accounts (accounts that cannot receive surplus get 0). Only one move is in flight at a time; while it runs the page keeps just the newest slider position. The locks are remembered in the browser. An account’s sheet does not show the surplus share; it is set only here.

Monte Carlo

The Monte Carlo runs on its own: after start-up and after every change of the settings, the Runs or the Seed, once the single run has finished and a further 1.5 s pause has passed. A change while it runs cancels it without a message and starts the new one when the engine is free; it does not run while the settings need a fix or while a comparison runs (it starts after the comparison), and it does not rerun on its own for settings whose run failed or was cancelled. Run in the bar above Summary and Plots (“Run again” when the result is current) reruns it by hand. Pick 25 (the default), 50 or 100 runs on Setup; the seed is the one Seed on Setup. The page estimates the time from the last measured run on this device (about 10 s for 25 runs on a phone). While it runs, a progress bar shows the runs done and the time left, and Cancel stops it within one batch (about 0.2 s); nothing from cancelled runs is kept and the last finished result stays. The result gives the median final worth and the chance of running out, the final-value histogram (leaving out the lowest and highest 1% of runs), the total balance band, the percentile table, and the middle half average per year (the runs between the 25th and 75th percentile of final worth) of the summary, accounts, taxes, deductions and RMD schedule tables and of the balance, cash flow, deposits and withdrawals, tax and expense charts. The app draws the charts and tiles; the tables go only into the Final report (PDF). The last two results are kept; when the settings, runs or seed change, the earlier result stays on screen, dimmed, under a note that the Monte Carlo updates on its own.

Compare

Compare always runs exactly two configs: two selects, Config A and Config B, each listing the current settings, the default profile (the example household) and the scenarios saved on this device under Setup; A and B are never the same (a deleted scenario is replaced by another option). To compare a strategy or a claim age, save a scenario with each choice. It runs the Runs set on Setup per config (Compare has no run count of its own; a run count an older page kept for Compare is ignored). The pair is checked by compareVariants with its rules and messages before the Run button is enabled. A strategy variant, claim ages or Compare chip stored by an older page is ignored.

Every scenario runs on the same random draws (the comparison’s seed); with no terminal tax rate set in the first scenario, each row’s own beneficiaries’ payout is taxed (see How Compare judges). Progress names the scenario running; Cancel works as for the Monte Carlo. The result has the comparison table (scrolling sideways with the scenario column kept in view; the beneficiaries’ payout or the terminal tax rate on one line under it, with the after-tax caption behind its “i”; its Median home and cars column shows the homes and cars less loans that Median final leaves out, and the “i” beside the Comparison heading says why the after-tax column can be higher), the total balance by age and year (the years of the first scenario; median lines with 10th-90th percentile shading per scenario), the final account value as one box per scenario. Each balance line ends in a dashed step past the last age to a dot at what its beneficiaries keep (the median; each series’ afterTax), named in the legend, tap readout and summary; the Show what beneficiaries keep box under the chart hides the marks, as on Plots.

Settings that differ (a closed card, with the count in its title) needs no run: it is worked out by compareDifferences as soon as the pair is picked, and again after any change to either config. It lists every setting that differs as its own panel, grouped by settings panel under a heading, with the schema’s labels: the setting, A’s and B’s value (an added or removed row, the partner or the visit-cost block has Show fields with the fields behind it) and two stacked buttons, “Merge A with B” (A takes B’s value) and “Merge B with A” (B takes A’s value), each with what it does underneath; from 720 px wide the two sit side by side. Rows of a list are matched by name, and a value under a switched-off block is marked (not used: … is off); a block off on both sides lists none of its settings. Turning a block’s switch off on a panel puts its other settings back to their defaults (editing.resetDisabledBlock). Settings whose values match but are held as an integer in one scenario and a float in another (for example 11800 and 11800.0, after the settings cross the page as JSON) are not listed.

A merge applies at once, with no second tap: mergeCompareDifference returns the target’s merged settings (an older saved scenario is upgraded first), and the page writes them to the saved scenario on this device or, for the current settings, to the working settings (the top bar’s Undo then records it too). A merge the checker refuses shows its reason and changes nothing. The example household is never changed: its buttons are off with the reason. “Undo last merge (n)” steps back through the last 20 merges; a config edited since its merge is left alone and the page says so. The comparison is not run again; its results show as changed until you press Run. With Auto-save on, a merge (or make-match) into the saved copy of the open scenario is also written to the working settings, so the next Auto-save and the next page load keep it (before, only the copy changed and the next Auto-save put the working settings back over it); an edit still settling is saved first, so the merge starts from it. Merges wait in line: a second tap before the first is back starts from the settings the first wrote, so both hold (make-match and Undo last merge are off until the line is empty). While a comparison runs, the merge and make-match buttons are off with “Off while the comparison runs; wait for it or cancel it.”; a merge whose config changed while it was worked out changes nothing and says so.

Settings that differ also has two make-match buttons, “A → B” (“Make B match A”, its accessible name) and “A ← B” (“Make A match B”); the arrow points the copy direction. A first tap asks for a second; the second replaces the target on this device: a saved scenario (an older version is upgraded first), or, for the current settings, the working settings (the loaded scenario stays the base, so Outputs shows them as edited). The default profile is never replaced; those buttons are off with the reason (matchProblems). The comparison then runs again and shows no differences.

Optimize

The fifth Outputs view, Optimize, searches the strategy settings for the best score (see Finding the best settings and How Optimize scores); the Strategies chip on Inputs starts with a quiet Find the best settings in the optimizer button that opens it. The run bar is hidden there, as on Compare. The card has one switch per search option (untick one to keep its current setting; its “i” says what it tries), the six for 401k deposits and new accounts among them; an option that cannot be used for the settings (optimizeAvailability, asked once per settings and seed) is off, greyed out, with Not available: <reason> under it, and is left out of the search. The beneficiaries’ payouts are not an option: each beneficiary keeps the payout set in Benefits, Beneficiaries, and Apply never changes it. The first option, Relocation state, is a list to tick instead of a switch: the states from appInfo as NJ - New Jersey in a short scrolling box, a search box above it on the phone (the select-search box, keeping the ticked states in view; this list keeps its search box because several states are ticked at once, unlike the single state comboboxes), None to clear the ticks, and at most optimizeInfo’s maxStates (5): with five ticked the other boxes are off under Up to 5 states per search. Under the list what the ticked states add to the chosen effort (optimizeStatesEstimate in frontend/src/shared.js, the same text as statesEstimateText) reads, for example, 3 states and staying put: up to 672 more runs, up to 2 minutes more (estimate)., the time from the seconds per run timed on the device (left out until it is known); with none ticked it reads Tick the states you would consider; staying where you are is always compared. The ticked states go with the options as states only when there are some. The second option, Filing status (couples), on by default, tries joint and separate returns for the federal return and every state return alike; without a partner it is greyed out with Not available: no partner. Its “i” warns that separate returns often cost more federal tax (Social Security taxed more, narrower brackets, lower limits) while some states give each spouse their own exclusions or caps, so the search weighs both; income is split between the returns only by account owner, as the model already does for separate returns. Risk preference (Growth-leaning, Balanced or Cautious, with what it means under the select), Effort (Quick by default; each label carries the estimate for these settings on this device as a range, such as Quick (estimate 25 s to 40 s), from the estimate that optimizeAvailability answers with (searchEstimates: the runs counted from the options the settings allow and a two-run calibration timed in the worker), and (up to 650 runs) until it comes; see the estimates), the not-advice note and Find best settings. The search runs in the worker as one batched job (optimize, startOptimize) with a bar of the runs done against the runs expected (the progress expected), the step, the settings tried, the runs and the time left (optimizeStepText and optimizeSecondsLeft in frontend/src/shared.js: the time per run so far times the expected runs left, OptimizeBatches.expectedRuns), and Stop, show best so far (the worker’s finish message keeps the best so far) and Cancel (nothing kept). At its run budget the search pauses (its progress capped, with capText) and the progress asks Continue (the worker’s answerCap message raises the budget and the same search carries on) or Stop and keep best so far; it asks again at each new budget until the search finishes. An automatic Monte Carlo waits while it runs; an edit drops a running search. A locked surplus slider keeps its share.

A browser app cannot run while the phone sleeps or another app is in front (the page and its worker freeze), so the help text asks you to keep the app open. While a search runs the page holds a Screen Wake Lock (where the browser has one) so the screen stays on, and lets it go when the search ends. Leaving the app pauses the search after its current batch (the worker’s pause message; the time away counts toward neither the time cap nor the time shown) with Paused while the app was in the background; coming back takes the screen lock again, resumes it (resume) and shows Resumed. The search’s checkpoint (OptimizeBatches.checkpoint: the outcome of every run so far with a hash of its setting, the code version, the run budget, the time spent and an early stop) is kept on the device under a key of the inputs, options, seed and build id (optimizeCheckpointKey); the worker builds one at most every 5 seconds, at the run budget and when the search pauses. If the browser closed the app mid-search, Optimize offers Resume the search (same settings and build only) or Discard; a resumed search replays the runs it kept, simulates none twice and ends with the result of a search never interrupted, a stop at the time cap or the run budget included. A checkpoint made by other code, or whose runs were of other settings, is refused with The saved search was made by another version of the app; start a new search. The checkpoint is cleared when the search ends, is cancelled or the settings change.

The result card shows, under Settings that change, one list of every change Apply writes (improvements, from improvementCards), one card each: the setting in plain words, its value now → the recommended value (wrapping on a phone, no wide table), and its gain. A change that improves the score is highlighted, its gain, such as +$15,247 score, in the accent and positive colours of the theme, light or dark. The gain is the score that change’s accepted steps added during the search (SearchResult.steps: the winner’s score less the incumbent’s on that step’s runs), so it is measured with the settings as they were then; a change with no measurable gain says gain not measured on its own; part of the total, and a change whose steps added nothing is a plain card marked changed, no measured gain (improves false). Under the cards Total improvement: +$N score, over M runs notes that each gain was measured when the search took that change, so the gains need not add up to it. The settings the search kept fold into one quiet line, N other settings kept as they are, that opens on a tap to list them with their values. Then the score breakdown, whether the best held up on fresh draws, the skipped strategies and notes; every text is built in Python. Apply these settings changes the settings at once through applyOptimum (only the settings the search moves), as one Undo step, and shows Applied N settings, naming the accounts it added and a 401k raise (applyText), with an Undo button. A Relocation state result writes the Relocation switch and state, so the Strategies panel’s Relocation section shows the move, and the state of each home and rent period starting at or after the move (homes.<n>.state, rents.<n>.state, listed as Home Villa: state); a Filing status result writes filingStatus (Joint or Separate in its card). It is off, with the reason, once the settings changed since the search (the result is then dimmed under a note) or when nothing changes.

Final report (PDF)

Final report (PDF) in the menu opens a picker of the saved scenarios (other than the open one): tick up to four to compare with the current settings, or none, then Create report (or Cancel). The current settings run their Monte Carlo first when it is missing or stale (the result is kept as the Outputs’ own); each ticked scenario then runs once with the same runs and seed, with progress and Cancel in the dialog. The PDF (US Letter) downloads as scenario-report-date.pdf, and the dialog says so; settings that need a fix show their message instead. The body, at most four pages, holds the headline figures, four key results (chance of success, median, 10th and 90th percentile final worth), the Insights (see Insights) and the charts (total balance, final values, balances, cash flow, taxes, expenses), each with a one-line caption and a summary of two to four sentences under it (the Taxes caption says the hatched bar is sales tax, already in expenses, shown for records; its legend key is hatched and its summary leaves it out); with scenarios ticked, one Comparison chart (every configuration’s median and 10th to 90th percentile band), its summary and one line of numbers per configuration follow, then one line per ticked scenario with how many settings it changes from the current settings and their panels (for example “Spend down differs in 2 settings (Accounts)”).

Each summary is built by fixed rules from the run’s own data (the charts and the yearly table), never written by hand. The rules find:

  • year-over-year jumps (a rise of at least 50% and $5,000) and drops (a fall of at least 20% and $5,000) in taxes and spending;

  • a balance reaching zero (at or below $1): the median or 10th percentile balance, or an account drained;

  • pay stopping and Social Security or withdrawals starting;

  • the peak, the end value, the largest category and the spread of final values.

A cause is named only when the yearly table shows it in the same year: a home sale’s gain, a sale’s proceeds, Roth conversions or required distributions beginning, pay stopping the year before (spending then drops “at retirement”), or, for the first account drained under the PRIORITY withdrawal order, every account before it already empty. Otherwise the summary says what happened without a cause.

The addendum holds the tables in small type with the header row repeated on each page, grouped under headings and subheadings: Your settings (Single settings by group, Lists), Year by year results (middle half average of the runs) (Cash flow by type, Accounts, Deductions), End of the plan (Range of outcomes, What beneficiaries receive) and, with scenarios ticked, Comparison (Settings that differ, then Results); a heading with no tables is left out. Settings that differ reuses Compare’s own list (compareDifferences of the current settings against each ticked scenario): one table per settings panel (Settings that differ: Accounts and so on, in the panels’ order), a row per setting that differs in any configuration, with Compare’s label, and a column per configuration with its value (the current settings’ value where a scenario does not differ there); with many configurations it splits into blocks like any wide table. Each heading has a short intro line, and each table a one or two sentence brief under its title saying what it shows and how to read it: one row per year led by your age, amounts in today’s dollars or inflation adjusted as the run is, or settings as entered in the app. A heading, its brief and the table’s header row and first rows always start on the same page. Every table spans the full page width. A table too wide for the page is split into as few blocks as fit, measured from their text, with the columns spread so the blocks are about equally wide, each repeating the row label column; the brief is shown once, over the first block. The settings tables leave out every field the app hides now, by the app’s own rules: a switch’s figures while it is off (a car’s loan figures without a loan, or with a balance of 0, and its commute figures with the commute off), the figures a switch replaces, a titled section whose switch is off, and a field whose panel condition is not met. The tables are: one table of single settings per group (Settings: You, Settings: Partner and so on), every list of items (expenses, accounts, loans, homes, cars, children and so on), the yearly table split into column groups (income, taxes and deductions, deposits and withdrawals, loans, homes and cars, expenses; the taxes group heads Sales Tax (records) as Sales tax (already in expenses, shown for records)), the accounts, deductions and RMD tables, the final worth by percentile, the Inheritance table (median Traditional, Roth and taxable money and homes left, and each tax: beneficiaries’ income tax, federal and state estate tax, inheritance tax, selling costs, what beneficiaries keep; each row its own median) and, with scenarios ticked, the numbers per configuration. Each page’s footer gives the scenario name, the page number and “Estimates for education only; not financial, tax or legal advice.” frontend/src/report.js builds the report data (buildReport, tested in Node) and draws it with jsPDF (drawReport); the charts are the page’s own SVG in the light colors, drawn to PNG at twice the size, so each chart by year shows your age over the calendar year as on Plots. jsPDF 4.2.1 is shipped unchanged from frontend/vendor (MIT; listed in legal/NOTICE.md with the libraries it bundles), so the report works offline.

Insights

The Insights card on Outputs, Summary (below the tiles) and the Insights section of the report (after the key results) list up to five warnings and up to five opportunities, drawn by fixed rules from the last Monte Carlo (the median balance band, the middle half average charts and yearly table) and the inputs it ran on. Warnings are ranked by severity, opportunities by dollar size; a kind with no items is left out, and with none at all the card and the section are not shown. The card ends with “Estimates from this plan’s own numbers; not financial advice.” Every dollar figure is rounded to two significant figures and every rate to a whole percent (returns to one decimal).

Warnings, each with its age:

  • the median balance falling 3 or more years in a row (the longest such run);

  • in a year without pay, the median balance of the accounts (homes and cars are not in it) below 2 years of that year’s spending;

  • in a year without pay, withdrawals above 5% of the accounts’ balance at the end of the year before;

  • a chance of running out above 10%, with the age the median (or else the 10th percentile) runs out;

  • the largest one-year rise in total taxes of at least 50% and $5,000, with its cause when the yearly table shows one that year (a home sale’s gain, a sale, Roth conversions or required minimum distributions starting);

  • an account drained while one earlier in the PRIORITY withdrawal order still holds money (with PROPORTIONAL, while any other account does);

  • years where spending and taxes exceed income plus withdrawals, so some goes unpaid (the yearly Shortfall).

Opportunities use simple arithmetic at the plan’s own expected real return (the accounts’ expected returns weighted by today’s balances, less the mean of the inflation minimum, mode and maximum), never a new simulation, and are stated in today’s dollars up to your retirement (the age your pay ends; the plan’s last age once retired):

  • cutting the largest expense category today by 10%, the saving invested each year;

  • what each $1,000 a year saved before retirement grows to (only while you work);

  • an employer match not fully used: the extra match a year at today’s pay when the plan deposits less of pay than the employer matches up to, grown to the year that person’s pay ends;

  • cash (Checking, Savings, CD & Bonds) above a 6-month reserve of today’s spending, when it is expected to earn less than the Investments account: the difference the two returns make;

  • the three largest expense categories today and their share of spending.

The wording is factual (“would add about”), never advice. frontend/src/insights.js holds the rules as small pure functions with named thresholds (tested in Node); report.js and app.js both call its insights.

App limits

  • The page needs WebAssembly in a worker; it does not load anything from a CDN.

  • First load is about 20 MB; the host serves the files compressed and cached.

  • A phone run takes about 0.2 s, so the single run behind the headline figures updates while you type. Runs offers 25 (the default), 50 or 100, about 10 s for 25 runs on a phone; Compare runs exactly two configs, each with the same Runs. Compare runs only on its Run button.

  • A scenario file holds at most 50 accounts, 50 loans, 20 cars, 20 homes, 20 children, 100 salary-history years, 200 expense rows and 200 rows in any other list, and only finite numbers (see Scenarios on Setup).

  • Saved scenarios live in this browser only (at most 50); export a scenario file to keep one elsewhere.

  • Settings are kept per browser and per device. Export a scenario file to move them to another device.

  • Wide tables scroll sideways inside their own box, with the first column (age or year) kept in view and a shadow on the right edge while more columns lie beyond it.

  • The page itself (index.html) keeps its name; if a phone shows the old version after a republish, reload the page.
