Walk Me Through It
  • Overview

Overview

Walk Me Through It (WMTI) is a planner for a household’s money over many years. You describe the household once: ages, pay, accounts, spending, homes, cars, loans and benefits. The model then plays the plan forward one year at a time and shows what is left at the end, what you pay in tax on the way and how likely the money is to last. Change one setting and compare: that is the whole idea. It is a model of US tax law, not advice.

It is one web app, hosted on GitHub Pages (https://rthornton3i.github.io/wmti/), that runs the Python model inside the browser with nothing sent anywhere. The same page fits a phone and widens for a laptop or monitor (see Using the toolkit and The app). Start here: this page has a quick start, what each tab is for and the words the guides use.

Quick start

  1. Open the app. Open the app link (see Using the toolkit). A first visit starts the walkthrough on the example household (fictional figures).

  2. Answer the walkthrough. It asks the main settings one question at a time. Skip keeps a figure as it is; you can come back later from the menu.

  3. Read the headline figures. The median final worth and its after-tax value stay at the top of every tab; the chance of running out is on Outputs, Summary.

  4. Save a scenario, change one setting, save it under another name.

  5. Compare the two. Both run on the same random draws, so the difference comes from the setting, not from luck.

  6. Optional: Optimize. The search tries strategy settings (Roth conversion, claim ages, withdrawal order, where surplus cash goes, extra accounts) and shows the best it finds next to yours. Nothing changes until you apply it.

Sample results shows what the results look like for the example household.

What each tab is for

The app has three bottom tabs, Setup, Inputs and Outputs, with a chip for each settings group; on a wide window the three sit side by side.

Where

What it is for

Setup

The saved scenarios, the household (ages, partner, filing status, home state), the run settings (seed and number of runs) and inflation.

Inputs, Accounts

Every account with its kind, owner, balance and returns; the withdrawal order and where surplus cash goes.

Inputs, Income

Pay, raises and promotions, the age pay stops and the earnings history.

Inputs, Children

Each child’s years, daycare and college costs and the 529 that pays them.

Inputs, Home

Homes owned or bought later, rent periods and utilities, on one timeline.

Inputs, Cars

Cars owned, bought later or leased, on the same kind of timeline.

Inputs, Loans

Student, auto and other loans, extra payments, refinancing and payoff.

Inputs, Benefits

The retirement plan and employer match, the medical plan and HSA, the dependent care FSA, and the beneficiaries who inherit what is left (they set how the after-tax value is measured, not the plan).

Inputs, Healthcare

The yearly cost of care: a flat amount or visits and drugs.

Inputs, Expenses

Monthly bills and spending, major one-off costs and charity; the costs other tabs already charge are listed first.

Inputs, Retirement

Social Security (benefit and claim age), Medicare and how spending changes in retirement.

Inputs, Strategies

The Roth conversion, the Roth backdoor and qualified charitable distributions, each off by default; and Relocation, a move to another of the 51 states (50 and DC) at a chosen age, also off by default (see States: taxes, living costs and moving).

Outputs, Summary and Plots

The results: headline figures, tables and charts from the Monte Carlo runs.

Outputs, Compare

Two scenarios side by side on the same random draws, with the settings that differ.

Outputs, Optimize

A search for better strategy settings (see Finding the best settings).

Final report (PDF) in the menu writes the results, and up to four saved scenarios to compare with, into one PDF (see The app).

For what to enter in each settings group and the logic behind it, see Model rules by group.

Words used in these guides

Scenario

One complete set of settings, saved under a name (the Python API and Compare’s Config A and Config B also call it a config). A scenario file is the same settings as JSON, and opens on any device.

Monte Carlo

Running the same plan many times, each time with different random returns, inflation, raises and costs. The spread of the results shows how much luck matters. Each run is drawn from the seed.

Seed

The number that fixes the random draws. The same seed and settings give the same results every time.

Middle half average

The yearly tables and charts average the runs whose final worth lies between the 25th and 75th percentile, so one lucky or unlucky run cannot pull the picture.

Terminal tax rate

An optional flat rate charged on Traditional (pre-tax) money still in the accounts at the end. Blank taxes the beneficiaries’ payout instead: by default each beneficiary empties their share in 10 yearly withdrawals, taxed at their brackets on top of their other income (set under Benefits, Beneficiaries).

After-tax value

The final net worth after terminal tax, including home and cars: what the beneficiaries keep. The accounts plus homes and cars less their loans, then less the federal and state estate taxes, the inheritance tax, the beneficiaries’ tax on Traditional money and the cost of selling the homes still owned, as if sold on the last day; gains are not taxed, since beneficiaries get a stepped-up basis. It can be higher than the median final worth, which counts the accounts only.

Surplus

Cash left over in a year after spending, taxes and saving. It is deposited into the cash, Investments and 529 accounts by the shares you set under Where surplus cash goes.

Lane

One row of the ownership timeline. The first home lane is the chain of primary residences and rent periods; each other home has its own lane. A car lane is one chain of cars that replace one another.

Replaces

A choice on the timeline’s Add screen for a future purchase or a lease: the new home or car takes the place of one you own, in that one’s lane, starting in its sale year. “Nothing, a separate item” keeps it apart.

What it models

The toolkit projects a household’s finances year by year:

  • Inputs: ages, incomes, savings rates, growth and inflation assumptions.

  • Expenses: living expenses and debt payments by year.

  • Accounts: balances, contributions and withdrawals for taxable, tax-deferred and Roth accounts.

  • Projection: the year-by-year cash flow, taxes and balances.

  • Outputs: headline results for the scenario, including Monte Carlo runs.

Phase 1 builds this engine and the app on top of it. Phase 2 adds strategy tabs on top of the same engine.
