Note: Figures confirmed primary (Code of Virginia 58.1-322.03(5); 2025 Form 760 booklet, Age 65 and Older Deduction Worksheet; tax.virginia.gov/subtractions; all read 2026-10-24): up to 12000 per person aged 65 or older, reduced by 1 for every 1 of adjusted federal AGI (federal AGI less taxable Social Security and Tier 1) above 50000 single or 75000 married; a married couple both claiming it computes one joint deduction (24000 less the excess) and splits it in half, so it is gone at 62000 single and 99000 for a couple both 65 or older (people born on or before 1939-01-01 get the full 12000, not modelled). Legislated, not indexed. Approximation of the rule: Virginia allows it against any income, the model only against retirement income; the income-based reduction is turned into shares of the retirement income at the middle of each 4000 (single) or 6000 (joint) step, assuming nearly all income is retirement income; a joint return with one person 65 or older is treated like two (slightly too generous between 75000 and 99000). Separate filers test the couple’s combined income in Virginia; the model tests the return’s own.

Sales tax (sales_tax)

Confidence: Confirmed secondary

State sales tax 5.3%; local sales taxes add 0.47% on average (5.77% combined). A car purchase pays 4.15%; groceries pay 1%. Clothing taxed: yes.

How the model uses this: The sales tax on taxable spending and on a car purchase (vehicle_rate), the rate on groceries and whether clothing is taxed; a move to another state changes the spending by the ratio of one plus each state’s rate, and a car purchase by the difference of the two vehicle rates.
