NJ Division of Taxation 2025 Form NJ-1040 instructions (https://www.nj.gov/treasury/taxation/pdf/current/1040i.pdf, the newest published; the 2026 forms are not out yet; read 2026-10-24): tax rate schedules (page 63: every threshold, rate and subtraction amount matches, and the bases are the cumulative tax of the lower brackets), exemptions (lines 6 to 12), pension exclusion (line 28a) and exempt income (Social Security, 401(k) contributions); the rates, exemptions and exclusion caps are set by statute (N.J.S.A. 54A), not indexed, so the 2025 figures apply to 2026 unless the legislature acts. Income base and add-backs (state_base)

Confidence: Confirmed secondary

The state adds back Traditional IRA deposits and HSA deposits (the employer’s included): the federal return lets you subtract them, this state does not.

How the model uses this: The federal deductions the state adds back to the income it taxes (add_back), and how its income base differs from the federal one. More in the model rules.

Note: New Jersey excludes 401(k) elective deferrals from wages (N.J.S.A. 54A:6-21; 2025 NJ-1040 instructions list employer and employee 401(k) contributions up to the federal limit as exempt, read 2026-10-24) but taxes 403(b), 457, SEP and TSP deferrals (same instructions, ‘Certain contributions to pensions’) and does not allow a deduction for Traditional IRA contributions (GIT-2, Rev. 12/18: contributions to an IRA are subject to New Jersey tax, read 2026-10-24), so the Traditional IRA deposit is added back to the income the state taxes. HSA deposits are not deductible either and are added back too: the instructions exempt only Archer MSA contributions and say nothing of HSAs, and secondary sources (Newfront, Cooper Levenson; failed HSA-deduction bills S474, S526, S1415, A2142) agree; no Division page states it outright. The model treats every Traditional 401k deposit as a 401(k) deferral, so a 403(b), 457 or TSP deposit is undertaxed here; the after-tax basis New Jersey gives a Traditional IRA deposit is not tracked, so its withdrawals are taxed in full.

Sales tax (sales_tax)
