Income base and add-backs (state_base)

Confidence: Confirmed primary

The state adds back none of the federal deductions the model tracks (Traditional deposits, Traditional IRA deposits and HSA deposits): it starts from the same income as the federal return.

How the model uses this: The federal deductions the state adds back to the income it taxes (add_back), and how its income base differs from the federal one. More in the model rules.

The retirement credit (up to 450 each, for people born on or before 1952-12-31) is not modelled.

Note: Approximation: Utah taxes the federally taxable benefits but gives a credit of the tax rate times them that phases out by 2.5 cents per dollar of modified AGI (AGI plus tax-exempt interest plus Utah additions) above 54,000 single, 90,000 joint and head of household and 45,000 married separate. The model exempts the benefits at or below those limits and taxes the full federal taxable amount above them, so it overstates the tax inside the phase-out band (a cliff at the limit). The thresholds, the 2.5-cent phase-out and the modified AGI measure are confirmed primary on Utah Code 59-10-1042(4) as in force from 2026-01-01 (https://le.utah.gov/xcode/Title59/Chapter10/C59-10-S1042_2026010120250507.html, read 2026-10-24) and on the Social Security Benefits Credit worksheet (code AH) of the 2025 Utah TC-40 Instructions (https://files.tax.utah.gov/tax/forms/current/tc-40inst.pdf, read 2026-10-24). They are fixed in statute, not indexed.

Sales tax (sales_tax)

Confidence: Confirmed secondary

State sales tax 6.1%; local sales taxes add 1.32% on average (7.42% combined). A car purchase pays 7.42%; groceries pay 3%. Clothing taxed: yes.

How the model uses this: The sales tax on taxable spending and on a car purchase (vehicle_rate), the rate on groceries and whether clothing is taxed; a move to another state changes the spending by the ratio of one plus each state’s rate, and a car purchase by the difference of the two vehicle rates. More in the model rules.
